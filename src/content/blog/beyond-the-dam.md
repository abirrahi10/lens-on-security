In the photo of what seems like a simple dam, we can extract many concepts of safety, access, and network protection. The bridge, allowing people to cross freely above the dam, acts as a virtual private network, allowing the flow of safe travel for anyone who is permitted to 
use it. 

### What is a dam?

As we start exploring the dam itself, we should first define what the purpose of this creation is. A dam is a "structure built across a river or other waterway to control the flow of water." [[1](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/dams/
)] In an organization, the first line of defense preventing attacks and bad data from entering your business is a firewall, which blocks similar to how a dam blocks water.

The last part of the photograph we'll explore is the control room, which, although not the central focus of the photo, plays a crucial role in ensuring the dam is operating safely. Making sure you have employees who control access for other employees ensures the safety of the business overall.

We'll start by exploring more about virtual private networks (VPNs) and why they're so important. You've probably been told that if you want to secure yourself and your location from the internet, use a VPN. We use VPNs for a variety of reasons, including "Improv[ing] privacy, prevent[ing] ISP tracking, streaming without geo-restrictions, avoid[ing] censorship, prevent[ing] data/bandwidth throttling, and [to] save money on travel and more" [2] These are all great reasons to use VPNs personally, but shouldn't we know what's happening behind the scenes? Are VPNs really to help us? 

A VPN is used to create a private, encrypted tunnel from your device to the VPN service. From there, they communicate with the website you intend to interact with. Along the way, interceptors may try to read any data being sent to and from the internet to your device, but the tunnel is meant to keep you safe. As I'm writing this blog, I'm sitting at a cafe connected to an unprotected network, with a VPN routing back to my home network through WireGuard. It gives me a sense of security, knowing that if someone sitting next to me is attempting to hack the network, I'm most likely safe from any threats.

"While excellent for individual use, VPNs are also a critical component of corporate security and governance." [3] In a business setting, VPNs are non-negotiable, as "It establishes a secure foundation for collaboration, remote work, and compliance." [4] Businesses share tons of data daily, and it's crucial to ensure this data is sent and received securely without being intercepted and read by threat actors. Each day, "Approximately 402.74 million terabytes of data are created" [5], including your text messages, your emails, photos you send, and even what you search up at work. An important part of using a VPN at work is the ability to access specific applications and data that live on the company network. You can't access it if you don't have your VPN on, so how will someone else get access to that data?

VPNs are one of the easiest ways to protect yourself from becoming a target at work, no matter where you are globally. It hides your IP Address (your devices location on the internet), making you and your activity virtually untraceable. Don't try to mess with it at work; reach out to your IT department if you need help accessing resources; and if you don't understand why you need it, I didn't do a good job with this blog so far. The bridge on the dam allows secure and safe access to a location you normally can't access otherwise, making it a crucial part of our infrastructure, and it should be a crucial part of your infrastructure at work.
